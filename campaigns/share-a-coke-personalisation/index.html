Share a Coke: Personalisation as a Global Brand Experience

Standardised Case Study Architecture

GLOBAL CAMPAIGN CASE STUDY · BRAND: COCA-COLA · CAMPAIGN: SHARE A COKE

Share a Coke turned product packaging into a personalised invitation to connect. In Australia in 2011, Coca-Cola replaced its logo on bottles and cans with popular first names, making the pack itself a conversation starter and a potential gift.

The problem and insight

In a 2014 account, Coca-Cola’s campaign team explained that research suggested younger Australians liked the brand’s iconic status but did not always feel it spoke to them at eye level. Personalising packs with first names was intended to make the brand feel more personal and to encourage sharing.

The creative platform

Make the brand personal by putting people into the packaging. The execution changed the role of the bottle: it was no longer only a branded container, but also a personalised object people could search for, give to someone and share socially.

How it travelled

  • Packaging: popular first names replaced the familiar logo on selected packs.
  • Participation: shoppers could look for names and later personalise cans through activations.
  • Digital: the campaign included ways to share virtual Coke experiences and encouraged social conversation.
  • Local adaptation: Coca-Cola described markets adapting the idea, for example using nicknames in China and other locally relevant executions.

Reported outcomes

Coca-Cola’s 2014 retrospective reported that the Australian launch sold more than 250 million named bottles and cans in a country of just under 23 million people, and said the idea had reached more than 70 countries by that time. These are company-reported figures and should be read as such. The brand also announced a new generation of the campaign in March 2025.

What marketers can learn

  • Personalisation can turn an ordinary product touchpoint into a participatory experience.
  • Packaging can function as owned media when it gives people a reason to notice, keep or share it.
  • A strong global platform still needs local adaptation: names, language and social norms differ by market.
  • Build operational safeguards into open participation, especially when public submissions or customisation are involved.
  • Separate company-reported campaign outcomes from independent evaluation.

Editorial note: This case study summarises Coca-Cola’s own retrospective and campaign announcement. The reported sales and reach numbers are attributed to the company, not presented as independently audited causal proof.
